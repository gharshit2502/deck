The idea

Fiber, made into modern FMCG.

FiberUs is the company. Gut Filling is its first consumer brand — fiber-first functional nutrition delivered through everyday formats, starting in Mumbai.

Most people don't think about fiber day to day, and the products that exist feel medicinal. Gut Filling's bet: make daily fiber taste good, fit into an existing habit, and look like something you'd want on your counter — not hidden in a cabinet.

Where things stand

Idea and creative direction defined. No formulation finalized, no regulatory review done, no manufacturing partner locked. This is a pitch for the direction, not a finished product.

The problem

Fiber has an image problem, not just an awareness problem.

People don't avoid fiber on purpose — it's just never framed as something you reach for daily, the way protein now is.

Fiber is something I take when I have a digestive problem.
→
Fiber is something I include every day.
Fiber products are medicinal.
→
Fiber can be enjoyable functional nutrition.
I know protein — I don't really think about fiber.
→
Fiber is an everyday nutrition need too.
Who it's for

One clear consumer first. Mumbai first.

Narrow on purpose — the product and unit economics need to hold up before we broaden the audience or the city.

Working professionals, 24–40

Busy, sedentary, inconsistent diets. They care about health but won't adopt anything that adds friction.

  • Long hours, low fruit/veg intake
  • Want convenience over routine change
  • Put off by anything that feels medicinal
Primary — first launch

Families with children

Kids on low-fiber, highly processed diets. A real segment for FiberUs, held to a higher bar.

  • Extra scrutiny on sugar, allergens, dosage
  • Age-appropriate formats and claims
  • Separate R&D and regulatory track
Future — not in first launch
The brand

Credible enough to trust, fun enough to reach for.

Roughly 30% clinical credibility, 70% everyday appeal — a nutrition company's substance, an FMCG brand's look.

The challenger

Questions why fiber has to be boring, medicinal, or something you only reach for once a problem starts.

The friend

Plain language on pack. No jargon, no lecturing, no fear-based messaging.

The optimist

Makes the daily habit feel small and doable — "this takes 30 seconds."

Roadmap & ask

Idea stage. Here's what happens next.

Bootstrapped-style: small-batch prototypes and consumer testing before any big spend. Target — meaningful prototypes in 3–6 months.

Now

Validate the problem

Talk to target consumers in Mumbai, confirm the fiber gap and format preference before formulating at scale.

Next

Formulate with R&D

Small-batch premix and gummy prototypes with our professor collaborator — taste, dosage and stability first.

Then

Test and narrow

Consumer taste tests decide which SKU earns the first real launch — evidence, not assumption.

Later

Commercialize

FSSAI licensing, packaging, and a focused Mumbai go-to-market for the winning product.

What we're asking

Pressure-test this plan. Tell us what evidence — consumer, science, or economics — would change your view, so we can go find it before we spend on it.
